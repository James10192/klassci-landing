A student information system holds pupil records, the grades that decide whether a student moves up a year, and money that families often pay in instalments across a school year. A head of institution considering handing us all of that is entitled to know who we are before signing. This page answers it with what we can establish, and stays silent on the rest.

## Who publishes KLASSCI

KLASSCI is a product of **African Digit Consulting**, a company based in **Abidjan, Côte d'Ivoire**. The publisher is named in the footer of every page on this site and in the site's publication metadata.

**KLASSCI is a product name, not a company.** There is no legal entity called "KLASSCI": the company that publishes the software, contracts with institutions and answers for this site is African Digit Consulting. We write it down because the question comes up at signing time, and because a brand allowed to pass for a company ends up being taken for a larger one than it is.

KLASSCI is not a badge resold by an integrator: the team that writes the software is the team that installs it, trains its users, and answers when something goes wrong. There is nobody between the institution and the workshop.

Company identification details — legal form, registration, registered office, publication director — appear on the [Legal notice](/en/mentions-legales) page.

## Since when

The first institutions were onboarded from **2023** onwards. The product therefore does not come from a specification written at a distance: each capability was built in response to a situation observed in a working institution — a grade entry session that never finished, payment reconciliation done by hand, a report card that did not come out in the format the supervising ministry expected.

That history also explains choices other vendors do not make: a separate database per institution, pages designed to hold up on a slow connection, and a public enrolment portal that works from a phone.

## Who we work for

KLASSCI is used by higher education and secondary institutions in Côte d'Ivoire.

The only ones this site names are those whose **online enrolment portal is open to the public**: they appear on the [enrolment page](/en/inscription) and on the home page, with the logo and name they set themselves in their own instance. A visitor can therefore check them without taking our word for it — open the portal, and the school is either there or it is not.

**This list is not our client base.** It is the public part of it. An institution that handles enrolment at its counter rather than online does not appear, which says nothing about whether it is with us. We would rather show less and verifiable than more and unverifiable.

We do not publish an aggregate headline figure of the "N schools, N students" kind. That number cannot be verified from the outside — ours no more than a competitor's — and [our own buyer's guide](/fr/blog/choisir-logiciel-gestion-scolaire-afrique) advises against relying on it. The check that is worth something is to ask for named institutions of a size comparable to yours, and to call them. We provide them on request.

## What we know how to do

The product covers the full life of an institution's academic year, and the team knows the regulatory ground that goes with it:

- **The LMD system of the UEMOA area** — teaching units, constituent elements, credits, semesters, compensation, examination boards.
- **Report cards and transcripts** in the formats Ivorian authorities expect, with averages, ranks and honours computed rather than re-keyed.
- **School accounting** — fee categories, instalment plans, collections, reminders, receipts, financial statements.
- **Attendance**, recorded during the session from a phone.
- **Timetables and teaching-hour tracking**, planned and then measured against what was actually delivered.
- **Online enrolment and re-enrolment**, open to families from this site.

Three editions exist depending on the type of institution: [university and higher education](/en/universite), [secondary school](/en/college), and [online learning](/en/lms).

## What we publish, and why

We publish three verifiable things, because that is the only form of proof that survives being read by a head of institution who has already been let down by a vendor:

- **[Sourced regulatory guides](/fr/blog)** — opening a private institution, the LMD system, computing averages. Every regulatory statement is tied to the text that carries it, and points that no public source settles are flagged as such rather than filled in with a guess. These guides are published in French, the language of the regulations they analyse.
- **[Product documentation](/en/docs)**, in French and English, including the [API reference](/en/docs/api-reference) and the [changelog](/en/docs/changelog).
- **[A dedicated security page](/en/securite)** describing the actual architecture, limits included, rather than a list of reassuring adjectives.
