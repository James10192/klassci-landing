## September 2026

### Added

- **The LMD transcript can now use the ministry's official layout** — two-column header with emblems, semesters set in the margin, grade and decision carried unit by unit, and credits totalled across the year. A school setting picks between this layout and the application's own; schools already printing the latter see no change. The choice is fixed at the moment a transcript is issued, so a school that switches later does not alter transcripts it has already signed and handed over. The decision agrees with the student's gender, and a missing decision is never replaced by an invented one.

- **The enrolment sheet now carries the whole file** — full civil status, contact details, programme down to the domain, parents and guardians with their relationship and occupation, and the list of documents with what is still missing. One full page per copy, and a signature line for the parent or guardian. Deliberately absent: account credentials, internal reasons for rejecting a document, and cashier comments — those are staff notes, not information for the family carrying the paper home.

- **A blank form to fill in the waiting room** — staff print it, the student completes it by hand, brings it back, and the office types it in. The field order matches the data-entry screen so nothing is skipped, the lines are tall enough to write on, and sex and State-assignment status are ticked rather than written. It asks for the level and programme the student wants — that is what lets the school pick a class — but never the class itself, which is the school's decision. Levels and programmes are offered as tick boxes, since a ticked box is typed back without hesitation. Available from the enrolments list.


- **KLASSCI installs on a phone, and actually works there** — the app sits on a phone or tablet home screen, opens without an address bar, and behaves like a phone app rather than a shrunken website. Bottom tab bar with a pill that glides between tabs, sheets that rise from the bottom and follow your finger, pull-to-refresh, and a banner when the network drops. Each profile gets its own tabs: a student sees Home, Grades, Timetable, Absences; a teacher, roll call and grades; the cashier, payment collection.

  The first screens have shipped: the student home, grades grouped by teaching unit with credits under LMD, absences with a justification that goes out from a single sheet (reason, attachment, no page reload), the cashier's four-step full-screen collection flow, the receipt with its actions, and the teacher's full-width roll call. Every action still obeys its permission: what an account may not do is not shown.

  On a tablet, a side rail replaces the bottom bar and lists move to two columns. On a desktop, nothing changes.

- **Our schools' logos now scroll across the home page** — every client school appears with the logo it uploaded in its own KLASSCI settings, the one that heads its report cards. Change the logo there and it changes here within the hour, with nobody in the loop. The demo instance is left out: this wall shows real schools.

- **Online applications now carry your school's colours** — the "Choose your school" list shows each school's logo and city. Once a school is picked, the form takes on the visual identity it configured for its PDF documents: its logo, its header line and its primary colour, which drives buttons, links and active fields. Applicants can see who they are handing their file to, and recognise them. You configure your identity once, in your settings: it applies to your report cards and to your application page alike. If your colour would not carry readable text on a button, it stays on the identity banner and the controls keep KLASSCI blue.

- **Document tracking, student by student** — the enrolment record now carries a "Documents" panel where you tick off what the student has just handed in. A checkbox is enough: no mandatory upload, no form. The counter at the top says whether anything is missing, and what.

  A document belongs to the student first, not to the year: six photos handed in during first year cover three enrolments, and the screen shows what is left. The school decides, document by document, what carries over and what is asked for again every year. A document that does not concern a student this year is set aside with a reason.

  Three school settings: what to do with an incomplete file (block, flag, stay silent), whether a cancelled enrolment gives back what it consumed, and whether ticking at the desk already counts as approval. Where the person receiving the document is the one who judges it, one gesture is enough — that is the default.

- **A page to know who to chase** — "Document tracking" lists, for a given year and class, the students whose file is incomplete, with the names of the missing documents. Five counters at the top: enrolments examined, complete files, incomplete files, documents to claim, documents to review. Each row leads straight to the student's file, exactly where you tick.

- **Student photos can be taken with a phone** — a dialog opens right after an enrolment is created, while the student is still at the desk. Three routes: upload a file, use the workstation camera, or display a QR code to scan with a phone. The photo taken on the phone appears on screen; the desk approves it or asks for another — it only replaces the student's photo once accepted.

  The page opened by the QR code requires no account and shows only a name: the link expires within minutes, is good for a single upload, and says nothing about the file. On re-enrolment, if the student already has a photo, the dialog first asks whether to replace it.

- **Required-documents catalogue** — the school declares what it asks for at enrolment: label, original or copy, number of copies, validity period, and which programmes and levels the document applies to. A set of common documents can be installed in one click.

- **The Curriculum screen can finally add and remove a subject** — until now it could only adjust what was already there: position on the report card, semester, core/specialty status. Completing a curriculum meant leaving the screen and going through each subject's own record, one at a time; removing one was possible nowhere without wiping the settings of its other programmes and levels in the process. An "Add a subject" button opens the catalogue, flagging what is already attached; a cross at the end of a row removes it, and warns first when the subject carries assessments at that level.

- **Every subject now knows which semester it is scheduled for** — the same page lets you state, subject by subject, whether it runs in the first semester, the second, or both. A "Confirm semesters" button records the answer for that programme and level: "all subjects in both semesters" is an answer in itself, and is recognised as such. Until semesters are confirmed for a pair, nothing changes in your report cards. Once confirmed, they are read: a semester's report card no longer lists subjects scheduled for the other one, and grade tracking no longer expects marks for them.

### Improved

- **A fee restricted to one group of students is no longer charged when the group is unknown** — a fee reserved for newcomers applied whenever a record did not carry the "returning student" mark, including when the box had simply been left blank. Both directions now require an explicit answer. The trade-off is deliberate: better to miss a fee than to collect one wrongly, since a missed fee can still be claimed, whereas money already received cannot be removed without leaving it unallocated.

- **The company pages are reachable from every page** — About, Security, Privacy and Legal notice only appeared in the footer, and two of them nowhere at all. They are now grouped under a "Company" entry in the navigation bar, on the home page as well as on University, Secondary, Virtual classroom, the blog and the documentation. Those pages were also missing a navigation bar entirely: you arrived from a link or a search, and the only way back was a breadcrumb. On phones the four links are laid out flat in the menu rather than folded into a submenu that would have cost one more tap.

- **One navigation bar everywhere** — home, University, Secondary and Virtual Classroom each had their own, at different heights, and the virtual classroom offered no way back to the other editions, no language switch and no menu on a phone. A single bar now serves them all, each page keeping its own links. On a phone the contact button finally appears in the menu, which was the only place it could be reached.

- **The product demo holds still, and explains itself** — the three angled screens on the home page span endlessly: a screenshot or a link preview always caught them mid-turn. They now sit still, respond to the mouse, and each carries a label naming what it shows — LMD tracking, cash desk and payments, and the families' mobile app.

- **"Apply online" is in the footer** — the families' entry point lived only in the navigation bar, which is to say nowhere once you had read to the bottom of a page.

- **Grade coverage tracking finally shows for the people who enter the grades** — the banner announcing what is missing had been sitting on the grade-entry screen and the assessment list from the start, but two obstacles kept it invisible: its display condition only recognised the global academic-oversight permission, and neither screen passed it the academic year. The one person the figure is useful to was the only one not getting it. The scope is not widened for all that: a class a teacher does not teach, does not mark and did not enter remains off limits.

- **The figure refreshes after grades are saved** — the calculation is cached for ten minutes to stay fast. The banner would ask again without saying so, get the same stale answer back, and keep reporting as missing the very grades just entered. Saving now forces a recalculation; simply switching class still uses the cache.

- **An assessment scheduled for later no longer counts as missing grades** — a class fully up to date with its marking would read "25 missing grades" for a test planned the following month, which entry refuses to record before its date anyway. Only assessments already held, or with no date, now count.

- **Quick entry now says what it skipped** — a row left blank, with no grade and no "absent" tick, was silently ignored while the screen answered "Grades saved successfully" as if everything had gone through. The message now counts what was recorded and names the students still to be entered while there are five or fewer. Entry being progressive by nature, the tone is that of work remaining, not of a reprimand.

- **A grade reported as missing now says when it is a duplicate student** — the banner reported "1 missing grade" on a subject the school had in fact fully entered. Both were right: the class carried two active records for the same person, whose names differed by a single first name, and the grade was on the other one. The two records are now named side by side with their student numbers — and this matching is shown for the whole class, including when no grade is missing at all: that is the costly case, since two fully marked records move no counter and still produce two report cards.

- **"Teacher to confirm" no longer shows when the teacher is entered right next door** — the banner looked for the teacher to chase in the general timetable, and nowhere else. A school that does not fill in the timetable saw this message on every subject although it had entered its teachers on the report-card screen. Failing a timetable, those names now show; the source appears on hover. And when neither answers, the message says where to go and enter the information instead of suggesting doubtful data.

- **A class's Subjects tab, the subject configuration and the teacher configuration now show the same list as the report card** — those three screens gave three different ones. Two crossed a subject's list of programmes with its list of levels, a product that invents pairs the curriculum does not carry; the third ignored the parent core curriculum, so a specialty class showed fewer subjects than its report card carries and the teacher of an inherited subject could be entered nowhere.

- **The pre-generation check for a class's report cards is markedly faster** — it recalculated the entire grade-coverage picture on every call, which nothing displayed at that point.

- **Confirming a duplicate at enrolment now leaves a trace** — when the check flags an existing student, confirming used to bypass the check entirely, with nothing anywhere recording what had been waved through. Months later, faced with two active records for the same person, nobody could say whether someone had knowingly confirmed. Confirmation remains sovereign — it is simply no longer silent.

- **The “Core / Specialisation assignment” screen is now called “Report card blueprint”** — its name only described one of its four jobs. It also carries which subjects make up the report card, which term each one belongs to and the confirmation of those terms, and their order on the document. A title that is wrong by omission sends people looking elsewhere for what is right there. The page address is unchanged: any links you saved still work.

- **The blueprint synchronisation command now simulates before writing** — reserved for the technical team, it rebuilt the blueprint from the cross-product of two independent lists and wrote it without asking. Two lists can only describe a full grid: as soon as a subject is taught in one programme in first year and in another in second year, the cross-product also invents the two combinations nobody asked for. It could moreover only add, never remove, so it could recreate what a removal had just taken out. It now shows what it would do, and writes only when explicitly told to.

### Security

- **Removal of troubleshooting tools that were still reachable without signing in** — a folder left over from the project's early days exposed technical details about the server, along with maintenance scripts a visitor could trigger. It has been removed from every instance. Alongside it, signing in from the mobile app now enforces the same limit on password attempts as the web form, and the class list is no longer readable without an account.

- **The platform's third-party components have been brought up to date, and the check meant to watch them can finally ring** — KLASSCI relies, like any modern platform, on software components written by others. Thirteen flaws rated high or critical were sitting in them. One allowed executable code to be slipped into a page through formatted text; another could bring the server down by handing it a document built to keep it busy indefinitely; a third could bypass the rules deciding which outside services the platform agrees to call. **They are closed.** The more troubling part, though, wasn't the flaws themselves but the fact that nobody could see them: the automated check meant to report them was written in a way that made failure impossible, so it announced "all clear" no matter what it found. It now genuinely decides, and it decides on what ships to production rather than on development tooling. It also stops on an alert the reference database hasn't rated yet — an unrated alert is not a harmless one, and that is precisely what had let through a validation flaw in the keys used to sign notifications. When a risk has to be accepted because no fix has been published, it stays visible with its stated reason rather than hidden: accepting a risk and concealing it are two different things. Finally, the test suite only ran during code review, never on a direct release: it now runs in both cases, on the development branch as well as on each school's own.

- **Submitted documents are no longer served directly by the web server** — birth certificates and identity papers were written to a public folder. They now live out of the server's reach and open only through the application, which checks who is asking.

- **Changing a class roster now requires a permission** — changing a class's subjects, adding students to it or removing them required no particular permission.

### Fixed

- **"Cancel" always cancels in confirmation dialogs** — on screens that ask for confirmation (enrollments, payments, student record, subjects, grades, timetable), a quick click on the confirm button while the dialog was still opening was lost: clicking "Cancel", the close button or Escape afterwards still ran the action. The confirm button now responds only once the dialog is fully open, and "Cancel" always cancels. The dialog also announces its title and the label of its close button to screen readers.

- **Bulk enrollment validation now states what it actually does** (`/esbtp/inscriptions` → Validate selection) — the confirmation dialog promised to automatically validate payments that were still pending. KLASSCI does the opposite, by design: an enrollment whose payment is not yet validated is skipped, never validated in place of the cashier. The message now says so: only enrollments with a validated payment are validated, their students are notified, and the others are left aside.

- **Moving an assessment no longer leaves a stale average behind** — changing an assessment's subject, class or term did move the marks, but nothing recomputed the stored averages, **on either side**: the one being left kept a value no mark justified any more, the one being joined ignored the marks that had arrived. This was not a display glitch: the stored average **wins** over the marks, on screen and on the report card. Measured after a move: a student whose five marks give 15.6 was shown 15. All four move paths now recompute both sides. For batch moves, the recomputation is capped so the response arrives in time; beyond the cap, the response returns the exact parameters to re-run. **An average with nothing left to average is never reset to zero** — no marks left, or only absences: it is left as is and flagged, its fate remaining a school decision, and no zero average is ever created from absences alone. On the assessment page, the message says what actually moved (the class, the subject or the term) and leads straight to the report-card generation check for the class concerned, which offers to remove it — or, when only absences are left, a case that check does not see, names each student and subject with a link to their averages. Each link only appears for someone who can open the screen it points to; otherwise the message says who to ask. A recomputation that fails is reported on the page instead of a silent “updated successfully”.

- **Recomputing an average could write 0 out of 20 when the assessment's term was stored as "1"** — an assessment's term is written two ways in the database ("1" or "semestre1"), a historical legacy. The recomputation that follows saving a mark only looked for marks under one of the two spellings: it found none, computed an average over nothing, and **wrote 0 out of 20 over the real average** — 14.00 became 0.00, measured. Marks are now read under both spellings.

- **The term of a mark moved from one semester to another was stored in a format the maintenance tools did not recognise** — the remote repair tools wrote the label ("semestre1") where the rest of the application stores the number. As a result, the orphan-average cleanup tool no longer found the mark, considered the average unsupported, and **deleted it**. The format is now the one used by the rest of the application.

- **Orphan-average cleanup now respects the scope it was given** — the mark synchronisation tool can limit its cleanup to one class and one term. That limit applied to only one of its two categories: the first, covering averages whose subject no longer exists, swept the whole school whatever it was asked. Yet that is the one that deletes most widely, since its criterion depends on no option.

- **A student's civil-status fields are no longer greyed out for those allowed to edit them** — surname, first names, gender, date and place of birth, nationality and student number were locked on screen except for accounts with administration access. A registrar granted "edit students" could open the record without being able to correct a single line. Those fields now follow that permission.

- **The mobile app's timetable and upcoming sessions work again** — both screens asked the database for a field that does not exist under that name for subjects. The database refused the whole request, and the two screens stayed empty without explaining why. They show sessions and the subject name again.

- **Words now agree with the student's gender** — a girl used to read her name followed by a masculine participle on her own file and on the receipt her family takes home. Agreement applies where the word describes a named person; pricing column headers and counters stay masculine, which is also the form used for a mixed group.

- **State-assignment status shows again for bulk re-enrolments** — a student re-enrolled in bulk as not assigned had no badge in the list or on their file: they appeared as if the status was blank, when it is what decides the amount due.

- **The second-semester tab showed the first semester's weighting** — on the « Edit averages » screen, a subject's weighting was looked up without stating which period or which student: the calculation fell back to the first semester every time, and never reached the weighting inherited from the common core, which landed on a default value instead. This was not display-only — the screen carries that value into its form, and saving writes it to the database; both are now fixed. A third gap closes along the way: saving a mark created a result row whose weighting defaulted to 1, never the one declared in the blueprint, and that 1 was what the screen then showed. The recalculation now reads the blueprint for new rows. Rows already recorded do not move, right or wrong: a weighting of 1 already in the database may be your school's own choice or the old default, and nothing tells them apart — they are corrected when the report card is regenerated, or by hand.

- **An LMD subject no longer prints on a BTS report card** — on instances that have imported LMD curricula, a constituent element picking up a line in a BTS curriculum came out on the class's report card, even though the two screens for managing subjects both exclude it. The subject therefore appeared on the report card without being visible anywhere it could have been removed — that is what made the defect expensive, far more than the extra subject. The audit showed this was no isolated accident: eight screens could display, or allow attaching, an LMD element in a BTS context, including a class's "available subjects" panel, which listed them all. Attendance screens are deliberately left as they are: they can legitimately concern LMD subjects. **If a grade had been entered on such a subject, it stops appearing on the report card** — the grade is not deleted, but the report card must be regenerated for the document to reflect the correction.

- **A subject's comment finally shows on the report card** — the two screens concerned were reading a property that has never existed under that name. A missing property raises no error: it returns an empty value. The comment entered at generation was therefore always replaced by a dash, silently, on every instance.

- **"Duplicate entry" when generating a report card: a subject removed then put back no longer blocks everything** — generation ends by setting aside the subjects it did not retain. That removal is soft: the row stays in the database, invisible, but still occupies its unique slot. If the subject came back onto the programme, the next generation could not see it, tried to recreate it, and the database refused — permanently. That student's report card became impossible to produce.

- **Repeat or pass is now decided on the annual average, not the second semester alone** — the second-semester report card printed "Repeats the year" under an annual average above the threshold. A student at 11.20 for the year but 9.40 in the second semester read a decision their own report card contradicted two lines above. The setting still exists for anyone who wants to decide on the second semester alone, but that is no longer what ships. **Note: schools already open must switch the setting on screen** — where the settings screen has been opened once, the old value is recorded and still applies.

- **Generating a report card no longer erases the board decision entered by hand** — on a second-semester report card, the decision typed in by the school was replaced by nothing as soon as the automatic calculation did not answer. And it does not answer in manual mode, which is the mode that ships by default: regenerating a report card was enough to lose the decision.

- **Removing a student from a class now archives all their grades** — archiving relied on only one of the two ways a grade is attached to a class. Changing an assessment's class does not update the first one: the grades concerned stayed live after the student was removed and kept weighing on their averages elsewhere. Both attachments are now taken together, on archiving as on restoring.

- **The annual view of a core-curriculum class is no longer empty** — on "whole year", tracking asked for the second semester's membership only. A core-curriculum class, whose students all move into a specialty in the second semester, appeared there without a single student although its entire first semester had been entered. The annual view now unites both semesters, and each assessment only expects the students who were there in its own semester.

- **A second-semester cohort written "S2" is no longer read as the first** — the calculation that says who belongs to a class over a period recognised only three spellings; every other one fell back to the first semester without a word. On a core-curriculum class, whose membership changes between the two, that designated the wrong students — for a headcount, a rank, or a bulk report-card generation.

- **A subject withdrawn from a student no longer counts as a grade received** — the same marker serves two purposes: the school withdrawing a student from a subject, and the system recording that a student was not yet in the class. The latter were counted as done, and a class where half the students arrived mid-year could display "all grades received" without a single one being entered.

- **Ticking a programme on a subject's record really does attach it** — the form announced "updated successfully" and the subject appeared nowhere, because it was writing to tables the report-card screens no longer read. Two further defects in the same form are fixed along the way: it emptied all of a subject's programmes and levels on every save, and it reset the report-card position, semester and core status of the combinations it kept.

- **One semester's curriculum no longer overwrites the other's** — loading the second semester's curriculum after the first shifted every subject common to both from one semester to the other: it vanished from the first semester's report card, without a word. When a load contradicts a semester already written, the whole batch is now refused with the list of subjects concerned, rather than writing a choice nobody made.

- **Grade tracking no longer sends a whole class's grades to screens that display none** — the response carried, for every assessment and every student, the numeric grade, the name of whoever entered it, whoever corrected it, and the timestamps, including to screens that show none of it. Grades and their authors are now served only to holders of the global viewing permission; duplicate students remain visible to everyone who sees the banner — that is precisely what explains to a teacher why a grade is "missing".

- **A subject from the other academic system no longer skews averages** — an LMD subject that someone had created an assessment for, and entered marks on, was counted in a BTS class report card. Measured on a second-year Building class: one such subject carried thirty-four marks, and a 14/20 alongside a 4/20 produced an overall average of 10.67 instead of 14.00 — saved as-is on the report card. Twelve of the product's average calculations now apply the same rule: report card generation, the "Official / Current" comparison, the Results page table, the class statistics printed on the sheet, the academic path average on the student record, the student's mobile home screen, and the re-enrolment decision — which displays nothing but decides between moving up and repeating the year. **The marks are not deleted**: someone entered them, and what becomes of them is the school's call. Report cards already printed keep their value until they are regenerated.

- **The Results page's "Overall average" was showing a single subject's mark** — the indicator strip at the top read saved averages, which are stored one row per subject, and kept the last one read as the student's overall average: no weighting, no ordering. A student with 14 in a core subject and 4 in a secondary one displayed 4.00. The pass rate was computed from those same values. The strip now averages all of the student's subjects. Rank is no longer taken from a subject row either: that was a rank within the subject, not within the class.

- **The "Edit averages" screen is no longer locked behind a toll** — until now, any subject whose coefficient was not configured for the programme + level pair made the screen refuse to open. Worse, the outcome depended on history: the same student, on the same subject, got the screen if an average had already been saved, and a redirect otherwise. The screen now opens in both cases, with a coefficient of 1 and a notice saying so. The "Configure coefficients" link stays within reach. Saving is now all-or-nothing: on refusal nothing is kept, and the message says which subject caused it.

- **A subject from the other system stays visible and removable on that screen** — flagged "outside system", with no input field, and with its removal cross. Hiding it would take its removal button with it and make it impossible to take out — which is exactly the defect that curriculum removal fixes elsewhere.

- **Curriculum screen confirmations no longer go through the browser's own dialogs** — removing a subject that carries assessments chains two confirmations, and that is the common case. Browsers offer, after repeated native dialogs, a "prevent this page from creating additional dialogues" checkbox; once ticked, the next confirmation answers "no" with nothing to indicate it — on the one screen the offending subject can be removed from. All three confirmations now use the application's own dialog, which the browser cannot silence.

- **A batch of curriculum removals no longer leaves a partial state** — a failure on the third removal left the first two deleted, the rest intact, and an error that did not say which. The batch is now all-or-nothing, and can therefore be replayed as-is after correction.

- **The Curriculum screen fits on a phone** — its rows overflowed by a hundred and two pixels below 640 px wide: the subject name, the term buttons and the report-card position overlapped, and the removal cross ran off the screen. Rows now wrap instead of squeezing.

- **Loading a blueprint by naming its programme no longer hits the wrong one** — programme and study-level names are not unique; only their codes are. Two programmes sharing a name meant the blueprint was loaded against one of them, at the mercy of insertion order, silently. The code is now tried first, and a name matching several entries is refused with all of them listed, so that the school decides. The same rule covers mixed institutions, where programmes created automatically for an LMD track carry that track's name: a BTS blueprint can no longer land on one.

- **A re-enrolled student saw last year's averages as 0.00** — the “Edit averages” screen can pick up grades from the previous year, which is the case for every re-enrolled student. But it displayed the row before computing the average, so the value shown was zero. The defect was visible only there: everywhere else, saving a grade immediately creates an average row, which took over.

## August 2026

### Added

- **Transfer from another higher education institution** — the online application form no longer assumes every applicant is coming straight from secondary school. A question at the top of the "Your background" section lets applicants choose between "I am coming from secondary school" and "I am transferring from another higher education institution". The second option reveals five fields: the institution being left (required), the programme followed, the last level completed, the year of last enrolment and the reason for the transfer. Baccalaureate details are still requested in both cases — it remains the first document the school checks. Schools see a "Transfer" marker directly in their applications list, along with the previous institution, and the full detail in the application record; accepting a transfer application opens the enrolment form with the Transfer box already ticked and the previous institution carried over.

- **Online applications from new students** — school leavers can now submit an application from klassci.com: identity, address, chosen programme, secondary-school details, state placement status and a guardian's contact details. Each file arrives in a dedicated inbox where the school accepts or rejects it with a reason, and accepting opens the enrolment form already filled in with what the applicant entered. The channel is closed by default: each school opens it when it chooses, over the same period as re-enrolment.

- **On-site enrolment start date** — a new setting announces the day the desk opens. Applicants read it on their submission confirmation instead of calling the school to ask when to come.

- **Bulk bulletin export to a single PDF** (`/esbtp/bulletins`) — an "Export grouped PDF" button gathers every bulletin matching the applied filters (year, class, term, status, search) into one PDF document, **in the order you choose**: by class, name, student ID, average or rank, ascending or descending. The export **still works even when some bulletins haven't been generated yet**: those are simply left out of the document, you're **warned before downloading** (how many are included / left out), and a **summary page** at the front of the PDF lists the bulletins that weren't included. Perfect for printing a whole class at once, ready to hand out.

### Improved

- **The portal now says exactly why a submission was refused** — an enrolment already exists, an application is recorded under a different name, the school has already accepted the file: each situation has its own wording and its own way forward. Two of them address the applicant and a relative sharing the same phone number equally, and are written to stay true for both.

- **A rush on applications no longer closes re-enrolment** — the two portals now have their own limits, and browsing the list of programmes no longer uses up the quota reserved for submissions.

- **Guardian details carried over** — in the enrolment form, one button copies the guardian the applicant declared online and names what is left to fill in.

- **Per-term breakdown on the annual results view** (`/esbtp/resultats`) — for a student who moved from the common core to a specialisation, the Annual tab now shows one table per term: term 1 with its common-core class, term 2 with its specialisation class, each with its own subjects, coefficient total and average. Every table now matches exactly the rows it displays, whereas the screen previously mixed the annual average at the top with a single term's average right below it. "Passed" or "Failed" stays reserved for the annual decision, a term table simply showing whether that term's average sits above or below 10.

### Fixed
